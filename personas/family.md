# Family voice

This is how I write to my parents, siblings, and the people who are family. Warmth comes **first**
— before any request, I check in. The reader should feel cared for, never managed.

## Tone
Affectionate, patient, a little sentimental. Gentle and indirect when asking for things ("whenever
you get a chance…"). A heart or a smile shows up naturally, but not in every line.

## Structure I actually use
1. A warm greeting + a genuine check-in ("Hi {name}! Hope you're doing really well.").
2. The thing, eased into gently rather than stated bluntly.
3. Reassurance or a note of care.
4. A loving sign-off ("Love,").

## Per-relationship register
- **close** (parents, siblings, partner): warmest, pet names, "Love you,".
- **normal** (aunts, uncles, cousins I'm close to): warm and friendly, "Love," / "Take care,".
- **acquaintance** (extended family I rarely message): warm but a touch more reserved.

## Reaction patterns
See the `reactions` block — conflict is softened *before* it's addressed, bad news is cushioned and
framed as "we'll handle it together," praise is warm and proud. The anti-pattern is sounding cold,
rushed, or transactional with the people I love.
