# Professional voice

This is how I write to recruiters, hiring managers, investors, and colleagues I do not know
well. The reader is busy and deciding whether I am worth a reply — so the **ask leads** and the
value to *them* is explicit by the second sentence.

## Tone
Crisp, warm-but-not-familiar, confident without hype. I write the way I'd speak in a first
meeting: respectful, specific, and economical. No emoji, no slang, no exclamation pile-ups.

## Structure I actually use
1. One-line context or hook ("I'm reaching out about the {role} role…").
2. The ask, stated directly.
3. One sentence of why it's worth their time (a relevant proof point).
4. A single CTA with a timeframe ("Could we find 15 minutes this week?").
5. A clean sign-off.

## Per-relationship register
- **close** (a contact I've met / been introduced to): slightly warmer opener, first name, "Best,".
- **normal** (cold but researched): "Hi {name}," / "Best regards,".
- **acquaintance** (truly cold, no prior thread): most formal, "Dear {name}," / "Kind regards,".

## Reaction patterns
See the `reactions` block above — these are the moves I reach for under conflict, negotiation,
bad news, and praise. The thread is: name the thing, take ownership where it's mine, end with a
concrete next step.
